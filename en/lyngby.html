name: Handform (Dao Chuan) descriptions: - Learn the fundamental Tai Chi postures, movements, footwork and transitions. - Practise moving smoothly without unnecessary tension, coordinating the whole body. - Develop balance, flexibility and control through complex movements. - Slow, controlled practice combines movement with natural, regulated breathing and is regarded as beneficial to health. - The movements are not merely choreography; they contain the techniques and principles that are later applied in self-defence. perfect-for: building the foundation of the entire Tai Chi system, while developing health, relaxation, balance and movement quality. - name: Weapons (Bing Qi) descriptions: - Sword, sabre and spear make you apply the same principles with an object extending your reach. - Weapons provide exercise that differs from the Hand Form, stretching and relaxing muscles and joints. - Practise coordinating hands, weapon, body and footwork as one movement. - The different weapons place particular demands on qualities such as awareness, precision, resolve and control. - Weapon forms contain practical fighting applications, these are taught together with the weapon-forms. perfect-for: developing precision, coordination, focus and whole-body movement while taking Tai Chi principles beyond empty-hand practice. - name: Pushing Hands (Tui Shou) descriptions: - Learn to feel changes in your partner's movement and intention through physical contact. - Practise responding naturally and quickly rather than relying on pre-planned movements. - Maintain soft contact so you can continuously sense what your partner is doing. - Practise redirecting your partner's force, affecting their balance and exploiting openings rather than meeting force with force. - Train the Five Directions and Eight Powers as practical preparation for applying the principles of the Hand Form. perfect-for: developing “listening” sensitivity—learning to perceive an opponent's movement early enough to respond before their attack is fully developed. - name: Self-defense Applications (San Shou) descriptions: - Learn what the movements of the Hand Form actually mean when used against an opponent. - Practise dealing with punches, kicks and attacks coming from different directions. - Learn to avoid strength, use softness and redirect an opponent's force rather than simply blocking it. - Practise throws, sweeps, trips, locks, holds and close-quarter techniques as well as strikes. - Progress from learning individual techniques to combining them naturally according to the situation, rather than memorising “attack X → response Y.” perfect-for: turning the principles and movements of Tai Chi into actual martial ability and practical self-defence. # Pricing pricing: -
